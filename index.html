1. Chapter 7A — Photosynthesis

Where it happens

Chloroplasts capture light energy. Chlorophyll absorbs light in the thylakoid membranes. Fluid surrounding thylakoids is the stroma.

Light-dependent reactions

Occur at the thylakoid membranes. Water is split, releasing oxygen. Energy is captured as ATP and NADPH.

Calvin cycle

Occurs in the stroma. Carbon dioxide is fixed into organic molecules. ATP and NADPH help produce G3P, a precursor for sugars.

What affects the rate?

Light, water, carbon dioxide, and temperature can limit photosynthesis. Many CAM plants open their stomata at night to conserve water.

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂

Remember: Photosynthesis stores incoming light energy in chemical bonds. A food web relies on producers making energy-rich molecules.

2. Chapter 7B — Cellular Respiration & Fermentation

Glycolysis

Cytoplasm • Glucose splits into two pyruvate molecules. Net gain: 2 ATP.

Citric acid (Krebs) cycle

Mitochondrial matrix • Carbon dioxide is released, and electron carriers such as NADH are produced.

Electron transport chain

Inner mitochondrial membrane • Produces most aerobic ATP. Hydrogen ions drive ATP synthase; oxygen is the final electron acceptor.

Fermentation

When oxygen is unavailable, fermentation regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue. Yeast can form alcohol and CO₂; lactate fermentation forms lactate.

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy captured in ATP

Remember: Photosynthesis stores energy in sugar; cellular respiration transfers some of that energy to ATP. Plants use both processes.

3. Chapter 8A — Chromosomes, Mitosis & Meiosis

Chromosomes

Genes are segments of DNA on chromosomes. Human body cells usually have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs); eggs and sperm usually have 23.

Cell-cycle stages

Interphase: growth and DNA copying. Mitosis: nuclear division. Cytokinesis: cytoplasm separates to form cells.

Mitosis = growth & repair

Prophase: chromosomes condense; metaphase: align at middle; anaphase: sister chromatids separate; telophase: nuclei reform.

Meiosis = gametes

Two divisions produce four genetically varied haploid cells. Crossing over in prophase I exchanges genetic material between homologous chromosomes.

Mitosis → 2 similar body cells (diploid)   |   Meiosis → 4 varied gametes (haploid)

Be careful: Homologous chromosomes separate during meiosis I; sister chromatids separate during meiosis II.

4. Chapter 8B — Mendelian Genetics & Punnett Squares

Alleles are different forms of a gene. Genotype means allele combination (Tt); phenotype is the observed trait (tall). Heterozygous = different alleles (Tt). Homozygous = same alleles (TT or tt).

How to draw a Punnett square: Write one parent's possible alleles across the top, the other parent's down the left, then combine the letters inside the boxes. The four boxes show probabilities, not guarantees for exactly four children.

Example A: Tt × Tt

Both parents are heterozygous; T is completely dominant.

Tt
TTTTt
tTttt

Genotypes: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt

Phenotypes: 75% dominant (tall), 25% recessive (short)

Example B: Tt × tt

One parent is heterozygous; the other is homozygous recessive.

Tt
tTttt
tTttt

Genotypes: 50% Tt; 50% tt

Phenotypes: 50% dominant; 50% recessive

Dihybrid example

For AaBb × AaBb (unlinked genes, complete dominance), each parent can make AB, Ab, aB, ab gametes. Typical phenotype ratio: 9:3:3:1.

Beyond Mendel

Incomplete dominance: intermediate appearance. Codominance: both alleles visible (e.g., AB blood type). Polygenic: multiple genes influence one trait.

5. Chapter 8C — Gene Expression, Development & Ethics

Turning genes on or off

Gene expression uses information in a gene to make a functional product. Promoters help start transcription, while enhancers and silencers influence its activity.

Environment matters

Hormones, light, temperature, and chemicals can affect expression. Cells with similar DNA perform different jobs because different genes are active.

Differentiation & stem cells

Differentiation creates specialized cells. Totipotent cells can form all cell types including extraembryonic tissues; pluripotent cells can form almost all body cell types.

Science and human dignity

Genetic knowledge can help diagnose disorders and guide treatment. Christians can apply wisdom, humility, stewardship, and respect for all people made in God's image (Genesis 1:27).

Key connection: Same DNA ≠ identical gene activity in every cell.
