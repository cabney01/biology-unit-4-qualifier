1. Chapter 7A — Photosynthesis

Where it happens

Chloroplasts capture light energy. Chlorophyll absorbs light in the thylakoid membranes. Fluid surrounding thylakoids is the stroma.

Light-dependent reactions

Occur at the thylakoid membranes. Water is split, releasing oxygen. Energy is captured as ATP and NADPH.

Calvin cycle

Occurs in the stroma. Carbon dioxide is fixed into organic molecules. ATP and NADPH help produce G3P, a precursor for sugars.

What affects the rate?

Light, water, carbon dioxide, and temperature can limit photosynthesis. Many CAM plants open their stomata at night to conserve water.

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂

Remember: Photosynthesis stores incoming light energy in chemical bonds. A food web relies on producers making energy-rich molecules.

2. Chapter 7B — Cellular Respiration & Fermentation

Glycolysis

Cytoplasm • Glucose splits into two pyruvate molecules. Net gain: 2 ATP.

Citric acid (Krebs) cycle

Mitochondrial matrix • Carbon dioxide is released, and electron carriers such as NADH are produced.

Electron transport chain

Inner mitochondrial membrane • Produces most aerobic ATP. Hydrogen ions drive ATP synthase; oxygen is the final electron acceptor.

Fermentation

When oxygen is unavailable, fermentation regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue. Yeast can form alcohol and CO₂; lactate fermentation forms lactate.

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy captured in ATP

Remember: Photosynthesis stores energy in sugar; cellular respiration transfers some of that energy to ATP. Plants use both processes.

3. Chapter 8A • Day 1 — DNA, Genes & Chromosomes

DNA carries the instructions

DNA stores genetic information. Genes are segments of DNA that contain instructions for functional products, such as proteins.

From DNA to chromosomes

DNA wraps around histone proteins to form chromatin. Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes when cells prepare to divide.

Chromosome parts

A duplicated chromosome has two sister chromatids joined at the centromere. Sister chromatids are copies made when DNA is replicated.

Human chromosome numbers

Most human body cells have 46 chromosomes, arranged in 23 pairs. Egg and sperm cells typically contain 23 chromosomes.

DNA → wraps around histones → chromatin → condensed chromosomes

Check your understanding: A gene is a section of DNA. A chromosome contains many genes. Copying a chromosome makes two sister chromatids; it does not immediately double the number of chromosomes.

4. Chapter 8A • Day 2 — Cell Cycle, Interphase & Checkpoints

Why body cells divide

Cell division supports growth, replacement of old or worn-out cells, and repair of damaged tissues.

G1: growth

During G1, the cell grows, makes proteins and organelles, and carries out normal cell functions.

S: DNA synthesis

During the S phase, DNA is copied so each future daughter cell can receive the genetic information it needs.

G2 and checkpoints

During G2, the cell prepares for division. Checkpoints help check growth, DNA damage, and readiness before continuing.

Interphase: G1 (growth) → S (DNA copying) → G2 (preparation)

Remember: Interphase is active, not a resting period. The M phase includes mitosis and cytokinesis. When cell-cycle controls fail, cells may divide abnormally.

5. Chapter 8A • Day 3 — Mitosis & Cytokinesis

Prophase (P)

Chromosomes condense and the spindle begins to form. As mitosis proceeds, the nuclear envelope breaks down and spindle fibers attach.

Metaphase (M)

Duplicated chromosomes line up at the middle of the cell, ready for the sister chromatids to separate.

Anaphase (A)

Sister chromatids move apart toward opposite ends of the cell. Each separated chromatid is now considered a chromosome.

Telophase (T)

Two new nuclei form around the separated chromosomes, which begin to uncoil.

Cytokinesis

Division of the cytoplasm completes the formation of two daughter cells. Animal cells form a cleavage furrow; plant cells form a cell plate.

Outcome

Mitosis usually produces two genetically similar daughter cells, each with the same chromosome number as the original body cell.

PMAT = Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase

Final check: Interphase copies the DNA; mitosis separates the chromosomes into two nuclei; cytokinesis separates the cell. Meiosis is not on this qualifier.
